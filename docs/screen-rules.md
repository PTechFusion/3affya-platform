## Rules

### Home and marketing pages

- **H1** (research) Put the value proposition, the differentiator and the primary CTA in the first screenful, and signal that more follows. Applies: Home, explainer, directory landing. Evidence: 57% of viewing time is above the fold, 74% in the first two screenfuls (NN/g eyetracking 2018). Source: NN/g, Scrolling and Attention <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/>
- **H2** (research) Write a one-line tagline that says what the site does and for whom; never a generic welcome. Applies: Home. Evidence: Homepage must work as an elevator pitch; vague labels have weak information scent. Source: NN/g, Homepage Design Principles <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/>
- **H3** (research) Front-load the first two words of every heading, bullet and paragraph with information-carrying words. Applies: All content. Evidence: F-pattern eyetracking of 232 users: readers catch the first two words of a line far more than the third. Source: NN/g, F-Shaped Pattern <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/>
- **H4** (research) Use real photos of the actual doctors and team; never stock people or decorative hero figures. Applies: Home, about, cards, profiles. Evidence: Users spent 10% more time on real team portraits than on bios taking 316% more space; stock people are ignored as filler. Source: NN/g, Photos as Web Content <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/>
- **H5** (research) Show up to 4 top-level links visibly on mobile before hiding the rest; keep visible nav on desktop. Applies: All pages. Evidence: Hidden nav: discoverability down 20%+, tasks 39% slower on desktop and 15% on mobile (n=179). Source: NN/g, Hamburger Menus <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/>
- **H6** (research) Show counts (doctors, specialties, bookings) only when they are large enough to impress; small numbers backfire. Applies: Home, directory. Evidence: A participant judged an article unpopular because it had only 1,000 shares. Source: NN/g, Social Proof in the UX <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/social-proof-ux/>
- **H7** (research) Put the three most-trusted credentials (licence, board certification, price) above the fold, ahead of any reviews. Applies: Home, profile. Evidence: Licence, board certification and insurance/price rated most important when choosing a clinician; reviews rated least. Source: J Patient Exp 2022, Choosing a Provider <https://doaj.org/article/30019e7bdeea4028b38558d51565cb57>
- **H8** (research) Define US board certified in one plain sentence wherever the badge first appears. Applies: Home, cards, profile, builder. Evidence: Only 23% of consumers could define board certification; 78% wrongly think it is required to practise; 91% rate it important once defined. Source: ABMS 2010 Consumer Survey <https://www.abderm.org/media/40431/abms-2010-consumer-survey.pdf>
- **H9** (research) Show a physical address, the operating entity, and an easy contact route on the home page and footer. Applies: Home, about, contact, footer. Evidence: Stanford credibility guidelines 2 and 5 (4,500+ participants). Source: Stanford Web Credibility Guidelines <https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines>
- **H10** (guideline) Language switcher in the header, each language in its own script (العربية / English), globe icon, never a flag. Applies: All pages. Evidence: W3C i18n: options in the target language, recognisable icon, keep on-page links even with auto-detect. Source: W3C, Language selection <https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-navigation-select>
- **H11** (research) Avoid false floors: never end a full-width section with a CTA and whitespace that looks like the page end. Applies: Home, explainer. Evidence: Illusion of completeness (NN/g); cut-off items and rhythm signal continuation (CXL). Source: NN/g Illusion of Completeness; CXL False Bottom <https://www.nngroup.com/videos/illusion-completeness/>
- **H12** (guideline) Target LCP of 2.5 s or less at the 75th percentile on mobile; compress doctor photos. Applies: All public pages. Evidence: Core Web Vitals: good is 2.5 s or less; most Saudi sites miss 3 s because image sets exceed 1,500 KB. Source: web.dev LCP; Namoun & Alkhodre 2019 <https://web.dev/articles/lcp>
- **H13** (research) Lead with US training plus US board certification as the unique signal. Applies: Home, cards. Evidence: On Zocdoc, non-US school graduates scored lower (4.63 vs 4.77); Gulf patients already travel to the US for care (USA is Saudi's top destination). Source: JMIR 2024 Zocdoc reviews; IMTJ GCC medical tourism <https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e51672>
- **H14** (guideline) Show a not-for-emergencies line with the local emergency number for the viewer's country. Applies: Home, intake chat, booking. Evidence: DHA excludes emergencies from telehealth; HHS: 911 only works in the patient's location. Source: Kayrouz UAE telehealth; HHS emergency plan <https://telehealth.hhs.gov/providers/preparing-patients-for-telehealth/creating-an-emergency-plan>
- **H15** (research) Address legality plainly: a short section on how a US doctor can consult a Gulf patient and what the visit can and cannot do. Applies: Home, FAQ, how it works. Evidence: Saudi survey (n=1,024): 60.5% had used telemedicine but only 41.9% felt the legal aspects were clear. Source: JMIR Formative 2023, Saudi telemedicine <https://formative.jmir.org/2023/1/e46446>

### Explainer, FAQ and content pages

- **X1** (research) Write patient-facing copy at grade 6 reading level; main message in the first paragraph, visible without scrolling. Applies: Explainer, FAQ, emails, summaries, chat. Evidence: Plain-language rewrite lifted task success 46% to 82% and cut time 22.3 to 9.5 min (n=50); AMA/NIH recommend grade 4 to 6; CDC CCI passing score 90. Source: NN/g Lower-Literacy; CDC Clear Communication Index <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/writing-for-lower-literacy-users/>
- **X2** (research) Single main column, static text, linear menus on explanatory pages; no moving text or fly-outs. Applies: Explainer, FAQ. Evidence: Same NN/g study: two-dimensional layouts and animation hurt lower-literacy readers. Source: NN/g Lower-Literacy <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/writing-for-lower-literacy-users/>
- **X3** (research) Make every credential claim verifiable: link to the certifying board or licence registry. Applies: Why US training, profile. Evidence: Stanford guideline 1: easy third-party verification raises credibility; ABMS Certification Matters is the public lookup. Source: Stanford guidelines; ABMS Certification Matters <https://www.certificationmatters.org/>
- **X4** (research) Show a last-reviewed date on medical, process, pricing and legal content. Applies: Explainer, FAQ, legal, profile. Evidence: Stanford guideline 8: visibly recent content increases credibility. Source: Stanford Web Credibility Guidelines <https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines>
- **X5** (research) Progressive disclosure, two levels at most: core steps first, details behind a labelled control; accordions for many short items, tabs for a few long ones. Applies: How it works, FAQ, profile. Evidence: Designs beyond two levels usually have low usability. Source: NN/g Progressive Disclosure; Tabs vs Accordions <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/>
- **X6** (practitioner) Keep the steps list short and highlight the recommended path. Applies: How it works, pricing. Evidence: Hick-Hyman law: decision time rises with number and complexity of choices. Source: Laws of UX, Hick's Law <https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/>
- **X7** (research) Show a curated set first (top specialties, a few doctors) with see-all, not the whole catalogue. Applies: Directory landing, home. Evidence: Jam study: 6 options converted 30%, 24 options 3%. Source: Iyengar & Lepper 2000 via Econsultancy <https://econsultancy.com/want-more-sales-give-consumers-fewer-options/>

### Directory, search and filters

- **S1** (research) Autocomplete: at most 10 suggestions on desktop, 4 to 8 on mobile; bold the predicted part; arrow keys copy the suggestion into the field. Applies: Search box. Evidence: 80% of sites offer autocomplete, 19% do it well; 58% fail arrow-key copy. Source: Baymard, Autocomplete Design <https://baymard.com/research-articles/autocomplete-design>
- **S2** (research) Tolerate misspellings and Arabic-to-English transliterations (specialties, names) in autocomplete and results; test symptom words like rash. Applies: Search. Evidence: 69% of sites fail misspellings; 90% of hospital finders failed the word rash and matched surnames instead. Source: Baymard misspellings; Kyruus via Becker's <https://baymard.com/research-articles/offer-autocomplete-suggestions-for-misspellings>
- **S3** (research) Visible search field with a submit button on mobile; persist the query in the field and the filter state in the URL so Back and sharing work. Applies: Search, results. Evidence: 21% lack a mobile submit button; 37% do not persist queries; 90% of load-more sites break Back. Source: Baymard search benchmark; Baymard/Smashing load-more study <https://baymard.com/research-articles/mobile-search-submit-button>
- **S4** (research) Default sort is diversity-weighted Relevance; never alphabetical or raw popularity. Offer Relevance, Soonest available, Price, Experience. Applies: Results. Evidence: 24% of sites fail the default sort; unrepresentative first pages made users leave; no tester ever chose alphabetical. Source: Baymard, Default Sort Type; Essential Sort Types <https://baymard.com/research-articles/default-sort-type>
- **S5** (research) Use Load more, not pagination or infinite scroll: 15 to 30 results on mobile, 25 to 75 on desktop before the button; restore scroll position on Back. Applies: Results. Evidence: Infinite scroll hid the footer and made users skim; pagination made users browse less; load more performed best. Source: Baymard via Smashing, Pagination vs Infinite Scroll vs Load More <https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/03/pagination-infinite-scrolling-load-more-buttons/>
- **S6** (research) Zero results must offer: relaxed-filter parent scope, 3 to 5 alternative queries with previews (auto-applied if only one works), popular doctors, and a human contact. Applies: Results, 404. Evidence: About half of no-results pages raise abandonment; spelling tips alone are not enough. Source: Baymard, No Results Page <https://baymard.com/research-articles/no-results-page>
- **S7** (research) Offer a filter for every attribute shown on the card; include price, language, visit type, gender, next available within 72 h. Applies: Filters. Evidence: 38% of sites lack filters for displayed attributes; 80% of mobile testers tried a price filter; half of new Zocdoc patients book within 72 h. Source: Baymard, Filters for List Item Info; 5 Essential Filters; Zocdoc <https://baymard.com/research-articles/have-filters-for-list-item-info>
- **S8** (research) Doctor gender is a first-class filter, suggested for ob/gyn, urology, psychiatry and dermatology. Applies: Filters, card. Evidence: Riyadh survey (n=3,015): women favoured female physicians for gynaecology, obstetrics, urology and counselling; men preferred male for urology, surgery, orthopaedics. Source: J Nat Sci Med 2019 <https://doaj.org/article/e3139a066bae43858880c1712e6cd4cc>
- **S9** (research) Multi-select within a filter type (OR), AND across types; checkboxes not radios; 5 to 10 filter types visible. Applies: Filters. Evidence: Testers commonly applied 5 to 6 filters, up to 10; 15% of sites block combining values. Source: Baymard, Multiple Filter Values <https://baymard.com/research-articles/allow-applying-of-multiple-filter-values>
- **S10** (research) Show result counts beside every filter option and on the mobile Apply button (Show 37 doctors); never let a combination land on an empty list. Applies: Filters. Evidence: Counts are one of the highest-impact improvements; they let users avoid zero-result combinations. Source: Baymard filter UI; NN/g Applying Filters <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-filters/>
- **S11** (research) Desktop: apply filters instantly (under 1 s, dim the list, keep scroll). Mobile: batch behind a sticky Apply in a bottom sheet; sticky filter trigger; top three filters first. Applies: Filters. Evidence: Four page loads for four filters is too costly on mobile; only 2 to 10% of mobile users open filters vs 38% on desktop. Source: NN/g Applying Filters; Baymard guideline 1125; Contentsquare <https://baymard.com/guidelines/1125-apply-buttons-in-mobile-filtering-interfaces>
- **S12** (research) Always show applied filters as removable chips with Clear all; never only a count like Filters (3). Applies: Results. Evidence: 28% of sites lack the overview; count-only overviews performed poorly. Source: Baymard, How to Design Applied Filters <https://baymard.com/blog/how-to-design-applied-filters>
- **S13** (research) Truncate long value lists at 6 to 10 visible values with a Show more (+N) link; never truncate a single value. Applies: Filters (languages, cities). Evidence: Under 6 values users assumed the list was complete; 15+ lost overview; inline scroll areas fail on 26% of sites. Source: Baymard, Truncation Design <https://baymard.com/research-articles/truncation-design>
- **S14** (research) List layout (one doctor per row), not a grid of cards, for search results. Applies: Results. Evidence: Cards de-emphasise ranking and are harder to scan; lists suit homogeneous, comparable items. Source: NN/g Cards; Baymard product tables <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cards-component/>
- **S15** (research) Breadcrumbs: Home > Specialty > City, one line, current page unlinked; parent level only on mobile. Applies: Results, profile. Evidence: NN/g breadcrumb guidelines. Source: NN/g, Breadcrumbs <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/breadcrumbs/>
- **S16** (research) Specialty names bilingual: Arabic first, English in parentheses; language filter names the dialect. Applies: Directory, filters, cards. Evidence: Gulf users code-switch for medical vocabulary; language-concordant care improved outcomes in 25 of 33 studies. Source: Diamond et al. JGIM 2019; Saudi code-switching studies <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31147980/>
- **S17** (practitioner) Track fill rate and zeros (searches with no bookable doctor) as the north-star; do not promote a specialty x city combination until it has bookable supply. Applies: Directory, ops. Evidence: Fill rate is the primary marketplace metric; 80% of marketplaces studied went supply-first; Zillow seeded from public records. Source: Lenny's Newsletter marketplace metrics; a16z; Andrew Chen <https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-most-important-marketplace-metrics>

### Doctor card and profile

- **C1** (research) Every card shows the same attributes in the same order: photo, name, specialty, languages, visit types, next available, all-in price, verification state; say unknown rather than leave a gap. Applies: Card. Evidence: 64% of sites show attributes inconsistently; testers rejected items missing an attribute their neighbours had. Source: Baymard, List Item Design <https://baymard.com/research-articles/list-item-design-ecommerce>
- **C2** (research) Visually separate each attribute (own line or style); whole card clickable, secondary actions behind a divider; key content at the reading-start corner. Applies: Card. Evidence: 40% of sites jumble attributes; unscannable info hurt almost as much as none. Source: Baymard List Item Design; NN/g Cards <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cards-component/>
- **C3** (practitioner) Headshot on every card; missing photo is a blocking profile defect; neutral specialty placeholder for unclaimed, never a broken image. Applies: Card, builder. Evidence: Profiles with photos get 60% more engagement (Healthgrades); about 2x views across 500,000 Doximity profiles. Source: Healthgrades provider profiles; Doximity 500k profiles <https://blog.doximity.com/articles/doctors-headshots-5-striking-lessons-from-500-000-physician-profiles>
- **C4** (research) Photo guidance: formal attire with white coat (scrubs plus coat for surgical and ER), plain background, no group or stock photos; a smile is optional. Applies: Builder photo step, admin review. Evidence: 4,062-patient study: white coat rated highest (8.1/10); no significant trust difference between smiling and neutral (n=268). Source: Petrilli BMJ Open 2018; Univ. Augsburg smile study <https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/8/5/e021239>
- **C5** (research) Show the all-in consultation price on the card and profile before Book; never a base price with fees added at checkout. Applies: Card, profile, checkout. Evidence: Drip pricing lowered trust and rebooking intent (n=1,000); 21% abandon when total cost is not visible early; 59% say price before care is critical. Source: ACM/OECD drip pricing; Baymard; AccessOne survey <https://oecd-opsi.org/bi-projects/full-price-information-for-consumers-what-effect-does-the-way-prices-are-displayed-have-on-online-booking-behaviour-for-holiday-homes/>
- **C6** (practitioner) Show next available slot and Accepting new patients on the card, live from the calendar. Applies: Card, profile. Evidence: One-third of consumers only view doctors accepting new patients; live availability lifted web booking conversion 30% (self-reported). Source: Healthgrades; Zocdoc case study <https://www.zocdoc.com/resources/case-studies/next-level-urgent-care-increased-booking-conversion/>
- **C7** (research) Show years in practice and training as the trust signal while there are no reviews; label New to 3affya instead of empty stars. Applies: Card, profile. Evidence: 61% preferred a 4-star physician with 20+ years over a 5-star with four; hide star averages under 5 ratings; 55% would still consider a provider with no reviews. Source: Healthgrades; Baymard ratings; Repugen 2024 <https://baymard.com/research-articles/user-ratings-distribution-summary>
- **C8** (research) Verification badge with a plain-language label (Credentials verified, with date), never an icon alone; name the board and link to the public verifier. Applies: Card, profile. Evidence: Verified-buyer badge raised purchase odds 15%; 56% of consumers do not know if their doctor is certified. Source: Spiegel Research Center; ABMS survey <https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/How-Online-Reviews-Influence-Sales>
- **C9** (practitioner) Order profile blocks: card data, credentials and training, fees and visit types, availability, bio; education is the second most-weighed factor after reviews. Applies: Profile. Evidence: Healthgrades consumer data. Source: Healthgrades provider profiles <https://ucmscdn.healthgrades.com/d6/24/00b66c3f4fa9941593d888fae713/best-practices-for-curating-provider-profiles.pdf>
- **C10** (research) Keep the Book CTA visible without scrolling on mobile (sticky). Applies: Profile. Evidence: Place-order and key options must sit in the first mobile viewport; users do not scroll far enough. Source: Baymard, Order Review; Guest Checkout <https://baymard.com/research-articles/make-guest-checkout-prominent>
- **C11** (research) Only authentic scarcity (a real next slot); no countdowns, no only-2-left, no strikethrough prices without a stated basis. Applies: Profile, checkout. Evidence: 11.1% of 11k sites had dark patterns; urgency tactics trigger reactance and damage trust. Source: Mathur et al. 2019; Growth.Design GoDaddy case <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.07032>
- **C12** (research) Visit type (video or phone) is an explicit choice with a one-line best-for hint; do not default everyone to video. Note: Saudi rules may require video (Q1). Applies: Profile, slot picker. Evidence: Patients trusted phone more than video for confidentiality (72.5% vs 59.8%); phone was the top format for 46.5% (n=567). Source: JMIR Human Factors 2023 <https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2023//e50740/>
- **C13** (practitioner) Keep name, address and phone consistent with the doctor's own site. Applies: Profile, builder. Evidence: 74.5% of patients check that a provider's details match their website. Source: Repugen Patient Review Survey 2024 <https://cdn.repugen.com/src/resources/patient-review-survey-2024-v2.pdf>

### Unclaimed profiles and the claim flow

- **U1** (practitioner) Label unclaimed profiles to patients: Unclaimed, details from public US records, not yet confirmed by the doctor, with source and date; never let them look identical to verified profiles. Applies: Unclaimed profile, card. Evidence: Yelp Unclaimed tag; Google Own this business; Healthgrades auto-profiles carry stale data for years. Source: Yelp, Google, Healthgrades patterns <https://pinmeto.com/blog/risks-of-not-claiming-gmb-listings>
- **U2** (research) No computed score, star row, or quality signal on unclaimed profiles; facts only, with their source. Applies: Unclaimed profile. Evidence: Avvo was sued within nine days of launch over algorithmic ratings built from public bar data. Source: Browne v. Avvo (2007) <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avvo>
- **U3** (practitioner) Rank unclaimed profiles below verified ones so the first page is bookable; give them a Request this doctor path if shown at all. Applies: Results. Evidence: Zeros (searches with no match) are the liquidity failure to minimise; seeded supply is a bridge, not the product. Source: a16z network effects; Lenny's Newsletter <https://a16z.com/16-ways-to-measure-network-effects/>
- **U4** (practitioner) Prominent Are you Dr X? Claim this profile, free, plus a visible This is not me / remove link. Applies: Unclaimed profile, claim landing. Evidence: Zocdoc claim page pattern: preview, editable sections, free, benefits, removal link. Source: Zocdoc claim page <https://www.zocdoc.com/claim/su-ho-ap>
- **U5** (practitioner) Claim flow: free, show the already-built profile and what the doctor can edit, verify by NPI plus email OTP, promise you can always edit this. Applies: Claim landing. Evidence: Zocdoc and Yelp claim patterns; Doximity starts registration from an NPI stub. Source: Zocdoc claim; Doximity registration <https://support.doximity.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047630573-How-to-Register-on-Doximity>
- **U6** (research) Warn and prepare: claiming invites scrutiny; set up alerts and reply tooling at claim time (for when reviews ship). Applies: Claim landing, builder. Evidence: After owners claimed Yelp pages, average rating fell over 10% for more than a year. Source: FIU / Information Systems Research 2026 <https://business.fiu.edu/news/2026/claiming-your-business-page-on-review-platforms-can-have-unintended-effects-on-customer-reviews-study-shows.html>

### Forms and sign-in

- **F1** (research) Labels above fields, never placeholder-only; single column; mark optional fields, not required. Applies: All forms. Evidence: Placeholders strain memory and are skipped by screen readers; users ignored asterisks (eyetracking). Source: NN/g Placeholders; cxpartners eyetracking <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/>
- **F2** (research) Cut fields to the minimum; one Full name field; hide rarely used fields behind links; never re-ask for data already entered. Applies: Intake, checkout, builder. Evidence: Average checkout has 11.3 fields, 8 are enough; 42% typed full name into first-name; WCAG 3.3.7 Redundant Entry. Source: Baymard checkout fields; W3C SC 3.3.7 <https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields>
- **F3** (research) Group long forms into chunks of 4 to 6 fields with headings; show all steps with the current one highlighted; allow back and save-and-resume. Applies: Builder, intake, Stripe onboarding. Evidence: 15-field form regrouped into three chunks (NN/g); wizard guidelines; users want to know steps remaining. Source: NN/g Form White Space; NN/g Wizards <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/>
- **F4** (research) Validate on blur, never on untouched fields; re-check on keystroke once an error exists; clear the error the instant it is fixed. Applies: All forms. Evidence: 31% have no inline validation; premature errors made a user feel they broke the site. Source: Baymard Inline Validation; Smashing live validation <https://baymard.com/research-articles/inline-form-validation>
- **F5** (research) Adaptive error messages that name the specific sub-rule and the fix, next to the field, in text (not colour alone); never the word invalid. Applies: All forms. Evidence: 98% of sites use generic errors; some users took 5 minutes or gave up; WCAG 3.3.1. Source: Baymard Adaptive Errors; NN/g Error Messages; W3C SC 3.3.1 <https://baymard.com/blog/adaptive-validation-error-messages>
- **F6** (guideline) OTP: one field, inputmode numeric, autocomplete one-time-code, paste must work; never reveal whether an email exists; rate-limit instead. Applies: Sign-in. Evidence: WCAG 3.3.8: manual transcription fails; split boxes that reject paste are a cited failure. Source: Cloud Four OTP inputs; W3C SC 3.3.8; Baymard security caveat <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html>
- **F7** (practitioner) Replace the passive check your email screen with a provider-specific inbox link (Open Gmail). Applies: Sign-in. Evidence: A/B test: +7% email confirmation rate. Source: Growth.Design, Sniper Link <https://growth.design/case-studies/sniper-link>
- **F8** (research) Right keyboard per field (email, tel, numeric); autocorrect and autocapitalise off on names, emails and codes; inputs rendered at 16 px or larger to stop iOS zoom. Applies: All forms. Evidence: 54% of sites fail keyboard types; iOS Safari auto-zooms inputs under 16 px. Source: Baymard Touch Keyboards; Strahl iOS auto-zoom <https://baymard.com/research-articles/mobile-touch-keyboards>
- **F9** (research) No login wall for browsing; ask for sign-in only at the commitment point (booking, upload). Applies: Sign-in, booking. Evidence: Login walls are among the most annoying patterns; delayed sign-up gave Duolingo +20% DAU; 24% abandoned carts over forced accounts. Source: NN/g Login Walls; Baymard Guest Checkout <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/login-walls/>
- **F10** (guideline) Phone input: GCC country codes first, LTR entry, per-country length validation; collect a backup phone and the patient's country at booking. Applies: Sign-up, booking. Evidence: Codex phone rules; HHS: pre-agree who calls whom if the visit disconnects, confirm location each visit. Source: Wikimedia Codex bidi; HHS emergency plan <https://doc.wikimedia.org/codex/main/style-guide/bidirectionality.html>

### Booking, checkout and payment

- **P1** (research) Full price and what it includes shown before the final step; the three lines (doctor fee, platform fee, card processing) sum to the total; nothing new at review. Applies: Checkout. Evidence: 40% abandon for extra costs; 12% because total was not visible up front; average cart abandonment 70%. Source: Baymard Cart Abandonment; Order Review <https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate>
- **P2** (research) Linear flow: Review step with inline edit links, then a separate Confirmation; review must never look like confirmation. Applies: Checkout. Evidence: 11.6% mistook review for confirmation; up to 11% abandonment. Source: Baymard, Office Depot Order Review <https://baymard.com/research-articles/office-depot-order-review-experience>
- **P3** (research) Encapsulate the card fields in a bordered box with one or two security cues inside (lock, Secured by Stripe); 3DS on for all cards. Applies: Checkout. Evidence: 19% abandoned over card-data distrust (n=1,026); 33% of MENA consumers have been fraud victims; 3DS recommended for all Saudi card payments. Source: Baymard Perceived Security; Checkout.com mada docs <https://baymard.com/blog/perceived-security-of-payment-form>
- **P4** (research) Expiry as MM / YY with input mask; auto-space the card number; keep card details after an error. Applies: Checkout. Evidence: 72% mismatch the printed card format; 34% clear card details on error. Source: Baymard, Expiration Date Fields <https://baymard.com/research-articles/how-to-format-expiration-date-fields>
- **P5** (practitioner) Cancellation and refund policy visible on the checkout step itself, not only in terms. Applies: Checkout, profile. Evidence: Stanford: avoid surprises; common gap on telehealth sites. Source: Stanford guidelines; Sesame pattern <https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines>
- **P6** (guideline) Every slot in the patient's local time with the zone named (3:00 PM Riyadh, GMT+3), doctor's time secondary; store UTC; recompute each render because the Gulf has no DST and the US does. Applies: Slot picker, confirmation, emails. Evidence: Saudi, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain UTC+3; UAE, Oman UTC+4; no DST in the Gulf. Source: timedate.org Middle East zones; HHS <https://timedate.org/time-zones/middle-east>
- **P7** (guideline) Shade weekends per country: Fri-Sat for Saudi, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain; Sat-Sun for UAE (since 2022); never hard-code Sat-Sun. Applies: Slot picker, availability editor. Evidence: UAE moved to Saturday-Sunday on 1 Jan 2022; the rest keep Sunday-Thursday weeks. Source: Executive Traveller; Wikipedia Workweek <https://www.executivetraveller.com/news/uae-switches-weekend-to-saturday-sunday>
- **P8** (practitioner) Collect visit reason, patient age group and for-whom (self or relative) before confirming; let the doctor set lead time (2 h default) and age rules. Applies: Slot picker, checkout. Evidence: Wrong-fit bookings come from missing reason, age preferences and lead time (Zocdoc); Saudi families often take part in decisions, so ask, do not assume. Source: Zocdoc wrong-fit bookings; Alfahmi BMC Med Ethics 2022 <https://www.zocdoc.com/blog/facts/wrong-fit-bookings-lead-quality/>
- **P9** (guideline) Telehealth informed consent inside the booking flow, before the first visit, recorded with how and when it was given. Applies: Checkout. Evidence: HHS: consent is typically done before the first appointment and documented. Source: HHS, Obtaining informed consent <https://telehealth.hhs.gov/providers/preparing-patients-for-telehealth/obtaining-informed-consent>
- **P10** (guideline) Confirmation is the peak and the end: both times, join link, add to calendar, what to prepare (device test, private room, ID), expected lead time, how to change. Applies: Booking confirmation. Evidence: Peak-end rule; AMA pre-visit checklist (camera, mic, ~384 kbps, private well-lit room, ID). Source: Laws of UX Peak-End; AMA Telehealth Playbook <https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/2020-10/telehealth-implementation-playbook-appendix-i3.pdf>
- **P11** (practitioner) Never pre-select paid add-ons; label items Included or Optional; pre-select only what most patients want (reminders on). Applies: Checkout, settings. Evidence: Default bias used against users triggers reactance; opt-out defaults change behaviour about 5x. Source: Growth.Design GoDaddy; Johnson & Goldstein 2003 <https://growth.design/case-studies/godaddy-checkout-ux>
- **P12** (guideline) Accept Visa, Mastercard, Apple Pay and Google Pay and say so up front; expect mada-only debit cards to fail on a non-Saudi Stripe account; Apple Pay is the main Saudi path. Applies: Checkout, pricing, failure state. Evidence: Stripe UAE/US accounts do not take mada; outside Saudi acquiring, mada cards behave as debit cards; wallets plus mada dominate Saudi e-commerce. Source: Stripe UAE methods; Checkout.com mada; SAMA <https://www.checkout.com/docs/payments/payment-methods/mada>
- **P13** (guideline) Capture in full at booking (no auth-then-capture); refunds not voids. Applies: Checkout, refunds. Evidence: mada transactions must be captured in full; partial capture and auth-only unsupported. Source: Checkout.com mada docs <https://www.checkout.com/docs/payments/payment-methods/mada>
- **P14** (guideline) Price in the patient's currency (SAR, AED, QAR, KWD, OMR, BHD); KWD, OMR and BHD use three decimals, round to the nearest 10 fils (verify Stripe rounding). Applies: Pricing, checkout, receipts. Evidence: Local currency improves conversion and authorisation rates (Stripe). Source: Stripe currencies <https://docs.stripe.com/currencies>
- **P15** (law) Keep a Good Faith Estimate style breakdown in the portal after payment. Applies: Receipts, checkout. Evidence: US No Surprises Act: self-pay patients get a written estimate when scheduling 3+ business days ahead. Source: CMS, Understanding costs in advance <https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises/consumers/understanding-costs-in-advance>
- **P16** (research) Reminders (when they ship in January): 7 days, 1 day, and about 3 hours before; opt-in WhatsApp where allowed. Applies: Reminders, settings. Evidence: Digital reminders cut no-shows 21% to 15% (21 studies, 16,000+ patients); WhatsApp used by 94% in Saudi, 100% in UAE (NU-Q). Source: BMJ Open 2016 meta-analysis; Zocdoc cadence; NU-Q <https://www.zocdoc.com/blog/facts/appointments-cancellations-no-shows/>

### Patient portal, records and AI summary

- **T1** (guideline) View and download are the two primary portal actions; phone-first layout. Applies: Bookings, summary. Evidence: 90% of portal users view results, 70% notes, 32% download; 51% use an app; 57% of adults use a portal. Source: ONC Data Brief 69 (2022) <https://healthit.gov/data/data-briefs/individuals-access-and-use-patient-portals-and-smartphone-health-apps-2022>
- **T2** (research) Release the summary to the patient immediately with a worry-reducing framing line; add context to abnormal flags. Applies: Summary viewer. Evidence: 96% prefer immediate access (n=8,139); 7.5% were more worried, nearly half less; abnormal-result viewers 3x as likely to worry. Source: JAMA Netw Open 2023 via UT Southwestern <https://www.utsouthwestern.edu/newsroom/articles/year-2023/march-medical-test-results-online.html>
- **T3** (research) Separate, explicit consent before any uploaded record is processed by AI, distinct from the privacy consent; say where data is stored and processed. Applies: Records upload. Evidence: 46% want permission asked before AI is used on their records, 38% notification (n=3,000, Jan 2026); UAE law restricts health data leaving the country. Source: Univ. of Michigan AI consent survey; UAE ICT Health Law <https://medresearch.umich.edu/research-news/ai-healthcare-notification-and-consent-preferences-based-us-national-survey>
- **T4** (law) Label every AI summary as AI-generated at the top with a one-tap route to a human; show Reviewed by Dr X or Not yet reviewed. Applies: Summary viewer. Evidence: California AB 3030 requires AI disclosure and a human route; only 54 of 100 LLM summaries reached top accuracy, 18 flagged for safety risk. Source: Duane Morris on AB 3030; JAMA Netw Open 2024 <https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/california_passes_novel_law_governing_generative_ai_healthcare_1224.html>
- **T5** (guideline) Not a diagnosis, does not replace the consultation: in the body of the summary, not only a footer. Applies: Summary viewer. Evidence: Disclaimer rates in AI health answers fell from 26% to 1% (2022 to 2025); FTC: no AI exemption. Source: eMarketer on MIT Tech Review; FTC Operation AI Comply <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes>
- **T6** (guideline) Every statement links to its source page; explain every number (what it means, normal range); show what we know and what we do not; a something missing? link. Applies: Summary viewer. Evidence: CDC CCI items 15 to 17; FDA CDS: basis must be reviewable; 96% of OpenNotes patients understood notes, 6% found something missing. Source: CDC Clear Communication Index; OpenNotes JGIM <https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/pdf/clear-communication-user-guide.pdf>
- **T7** (research) Who can see this panel on every record and share; share expiry visible; revoke in one tap. Applies: Sharing and consent. Evidence: Over 12% withheld information from providers over privacy fears; only about 25% very confident in record privacy. Source: JMIR privacy survey via Becker's <https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/patient-perception-of-health-record-privacy-security-6-findings/>
- **T8** (research) Delete my uploads and Delete my account controls that state what deletion covers and what legal retention keeps. Applies: Settings, records. Evidence: 37% think AI will make record security worse; UAE mandates 25-year retention for provider records. Source: Pew 2023; Al Tamimi UAE ICT Health Law <https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2023/02/22/60-of-americans-would-be-uncomfortable-with-provider-relying-on-ai-in-their-own-health-care/>
- **T9** (law) Notice of Privacy Practices posted prominently, plain language, effective date, complaints contact; acknowledgment captured at first use. Applies: Legal pages, portal. Evidence: HHS: prominently post the notice on any site about services; deliver with the first electronic service. Source: HHS, Notice of Privacy Practices <https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/privacy-practices-for-protected-health-information/index.html>
- **T10** (research) Upload designed for phone photos of paper reports first; accepted formats, size limit and per-file success state visible. Applies: Records upload. Evidence: Portal upload adoption is slow when friction is high (53 uploaders in four years at one practice). Source: Applied Clinical Informatics 2019 <https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid30970383>
- **T11** (research) Show the work during processing: a live step list (Reading page 3 of 12, Extracting diagnoses, Writing summary); no fake percentage; over 10 s show an estimate and a cancel. Applies: Records processing. Evidence: Labour illusion: users preferred a slower site that showed its work; 86% of users preferred a progress bar; never let a bar move backwards. Source: Buell & Norton 2011; UX Tigers progress indicators <https://www.uxtigers.com/post/progress-indicators>
- **T12** (research) Confidentiality statement in plain Arabic at booking and in the consult lobby; never push health details through marketing channels. Applies: Booking, lobby, settings. Evidence: Worry about online monitoring rose from 34% to 44% across seven Arab countries (n=7,303). Source: NU-Q Media Use 2019 <https://www.qatar.northwestern.edu/news/articles/2019/12-media-use-survey-2019.html>

### Professional portal and profile builder

- **B1** (practitioner) Start from the pre-seeded stub (Find your profile), never a blank form. Applies: Builder, claim. Evidence: Doximity registration starts by claiming an NPI stub; about 70% of US doctors are on it. Source: Doximity registration <https://support.doximity.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047630573-How-to-Register-on-Doximity>
- **B2** (research) Required fields are only what admin will verify (NPI, licence, board, specialty, photo); everything else is complete later. Applies: Builder. Evidence: Practices spend about USD 2.76B a year updating directory data; 40%+ never correct their listings. Source: AHA on CAQH; Healthgrades <https://www.aha.org/node/402650>
- **B3** (research) Endowed progress: start the checklist partly filled from registry data and show steps remaining. Applies: Builder, Stripe onboarding. Evidence: 12-stamp card with 2 free stamps completed in median 10 days vs 15; Zeigarnik effect. Source: Kivetz et al. via Columbia Business School <https://business.columbia.edu/insights/chazen-global-insights/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration>
- **B4** (research) Live preview card that renders exactly as the public card; show the benefit at the step (photos get about 2x views). Applies: Builder. Evidence: NN/g heuristics 1 and 6; Doximity 500k-profile analysis. Source: NN/g 10 Heuristics; Doximity <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/>
- **B5** (research) Deliver value before asking for effort: show the claimed card and benefits before Stripe onboarding. Applies: Builder, payouts. Evidence: Fogg B=MAP; reciprocity. Source: Fogg Behavior Model; Growth.Design psychology <https://behaviormodel.org/>
- **B6** (practitioner) Structured fields, not free text: years in practice, board and status, training, languages with dialect, Accepting new patients (default off until availability is set). Applies: Builder. Evidence: NCQA primary-source verification needs board, licence and training as separate items; one-third of patients filter on accepting. Source: Verifiable on NCQA; Healthgrades <https://verifiable.com/blog/ncqa-primary-source-verification-requirements>
- **B7** (research) Board eligible is a distinct text label, never the badge; reject board certified claims without a named ABMS/AOA board. Applies: Builder, admin review. Evidence: 78% of consumers conflate licensure with certification; FTC treats unsubstantiated claims as deceptive. Source: ABMS survey; FTC <https://www.abderm.org/media/40431/abms-2010-consumer-survey.pdf>
- **B8** (research) Membership page: three tiers, at most four attributes differing between adjacent tiers, features carry upward, all-in price and billing period up front, static comparison table. Applies: For professionals, membership. Evidence: Good-Better-Best (HBR 2018); NN/g comparison tables; drip pricing harms trust. Source: HBR Good-Better-Best; NN/g Comparison Tables <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/comparison-tables/>
- **B9** (practitioner) Sell membership on patient-side outcomes with numbers: live availability (half of new patients book within 72 h), photos (2x views), fast response. Applies: For professionals. Evidence: Zocdoc patient access data; Doximity. Source: Zocdoc patient access <https://www.zocdoc.com/about/patientaccess/>
- **B10** (research) Prefer undo over confirmation for routine actions; confirmation dialogs only for irreversible actions, buttons that name the outcome (Cancel booking / Keep booking). Applies: Portals, admin. Evidence: Frequent confirmations train click-through. Source: NN/g Confirmation Dialogs <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/>

- **A1** (practitioner) Verify licence, board certification and sanctions against primary sources (state board, ABMS, FSMB/NPDB) before Verified shows; store source, date and reviewer. Applies: Credential review. Evidence: NCQA primary-source verification elements. Source: Verifiable on NCQA PSV <https://verifiable.com/blog/ncqa-primary-source-verification-requirements>

### Intake chat

- **I1** (research) First message: this is an AI assistant, not a doctor; not a diagnosis; the 3 to 5 things it can do; emergency number for the viewer's country; persistent Talk to a human button. Applies: Intake chat. Evidence: Users adjusted once they knew it was a bot; unclear scope left users stuck; AB 3030 human route; 60% uncomfortable with AI in their care. Source: NN/g Chatbots; Duane Morris AB 3030; Pew 2023 <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chatbots/>
- **I2** (research) Tappable quick replies for common answers alongside free text; tolerate typos; keep context across turns; allow going back; say I did not understand instead of guessing. Applies: Intake chat. Evidence: Domino's re-asked an address; No dead-ended a Kia bot; users preferred honest failure. Source: NN/g, The User Experience of Chatbots <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chatbots/>
- **I3** (research) Never output a diagnosis or triage level; collect structured history and suggest specialties only; hand off to the doctor. Applies: Intake chat. Evidence: Symptom checker RCT (n=437): top suggestion matched discharge diagnosis in 14%; over-triage 53%. Source: JMIR 2024 Ada vs Symptoma <https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56514>
- **I4** (research) Hard-coded red-flag detection (chest pain, stroke signs, pregnancy bleeding, self-harm) interrupts with emergency guidance and ends intake. Applies: Intake chat. Evidence: Symptom checkers miss life-threatening cases; HHS requires an emergency plan. Source: JMIR 2024; HHS emergency plan <https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56514>
- **I5** (research) Do not collect sensitive data (date of birth, card, full address) in chat; hand off to a form. Applies: Intake chat. Evidence: Participants hesitated to share birthdays, addresses and card numbers in chat. Source: NN/g Chatbots <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chatbots/>

### Transactional email

- **E1** (research) Subject states the content (Your visit with Dr X is confirmed, 14 Nov, 6 pm Riyadh); preheader carries real info, not view in browser. Applies: All emails. Evidence: NN/g diary study: uninformative subjects and wasted preheaders drew the harshest comments. Source: NN/g, State of Transactional Email <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-transactional-email/>
- **E2** (research) Answer every likely question in the body (what, when in both zones, how to join, how to change, what to prepare); no ads; works on a small screen. Applies: Booking, OTP, receipts. Evidence: Users annoyed by emails that require extra work or carry sales pitches. Source: NN/g, State of Transactional Email <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-transactional-email/>
- **E3** (research) Receipt mirrors the review step: total most prominent, the three lines, last 4 digits, both times, change and cancel links. Applies: Receipt, refund. Evidence: Baymard order-review information list. Source: Baymard, Office Depot Order Review <https://baymard.com/research-articles/office-depot-order-review-experience>
- **E4** (guideline) RTL email: dir=rtl on the table cells, Arabic at 16 px or larger, numbers and codes isolated LTR; test in Gmail and Outlook. Applies: All emails. Evidence: W3C inline bidi; Apple +2 pt guidance. Source: W3C inline bidi markup <https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/>

### Loading, empty and error states

- **L1** (research) Response limits: under 1 s no indicator; 1 to 10 s a content-shaped skeleton (onset after about 1 s) or a module spinner; over 10 s a progress bar with estimate and cancel. Applies: All screens. Evidence: Nielsen's three limits; frame-only skeletons make users think the page is broken. Source: NN/g Response Times; Skeleton Screens 101 <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/>
- **L2** (practitioner) Respond to every tap within 400 ms with at least an optimistic change (filters, slot holds, chat). Applies: Filters, availability editor, chat. Evidence: Doherty threshold (IBM 1982). Source: Laws of UX, Doherty Threshold <https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/>
- **L3** (research) Every empty state says what the container is, why it is empty, and gives one direct action; never show No records while content is still loading. Applies: Bookings, records, inbox, admin queues. Evidence: NN/g empty-state guidelines. Source: NN/g, Designing Empty States <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/>
- **L4** (research) Errors: exact problem next to the element, the fix, input preserved, no blame, no humour, no codes by default. Applies: All screens. Evidence: NN/g error-message guidelines; WCAG 3.3.1. Source: NN/g Error-Message Guidelines <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/>

### Arabic, RTL and typography

- **R1** (guideline) dir=rtl and lang=ar on html for the Arabic locale; the whole layout, nav order and alignment flip, not just the text. Applies: All screens. Evidence: Apple HIG and Material bidirectionality. Source: Apple HIG, Right to left <https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left>
- **R2** (guideline) Mirror: back and next, chevrons, steppers, progress bars (0% on the right), pagination, carousels, text-alignment icons. Do not mirror: media controls, clocks, search magnifier, pencil, checkmarks, logos, card-scheme marks, photos. Applies: All screens, brand page. Evidence: Apple HIG, Material m1, Wikimedia Codex, Microsoft mirroring guidance agree. Source: Apple HIG RTL; Material m1 Bidirectionality; Codex <https://m1.material.io/usability/bidirectionality.html>
- **R3** (guideline) Never reverse digits: phone numbers, card numbers, OTP codes, booking references stay LTR, left-aligned, wrapped in dir=ltr; use bdi or dir=auto for database strings (doctor and hospital names). Applies: Forms, confirmation, emails, cards. Evidence: W3C: wrap every opposite-direction phrase; Codex: phone numbers never mirrored. Source: W3C inline bidi; Codex <https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/>
- **R4** (guideline) Align 3+ line paragraphs by their own language (English bio inside an Arabic page stays left-aligned); 1 to 2 line labels follow the page direction; list items share one alignment even when some are Latin. Applies: Profile bio, credentials, legal. Evidence: Apple HIG RTL. Source: Apple HIG, Right to left <https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left>
- **R5** (research) Arabic body 16 px floor on mobile, 18 px preferred; about +2 pt when Arabic sits beside uppercase Latin; line height 1.5 or more. Applies: All screens, brand page. Evidence: Apple +2 pt; over 60% of 73 Saudi sites set paragraphs at 12 to 16 px, judged too small; Arabic extenders need extra leading (W3C alreq). Source: Apple HIG; Namoun & Alkhodre 2019; W3C alreq <https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume10No4/Paper_72-Towards_Usability_Guidelines_for_the_Design_of_Effective_Arabic_Websites.pdf>
- **R6** (guideline) letter-spacing: 0 on [lang=ar]; font-synthesis: none; real Readex Pro weights; no italics for Arabic (emphasise with weight or colour); no text-align: justify. Applies: Brand page, all screens. Evidence: Spacing joined letters creates undesirable results (W3C alreq); browsers lack kashida justification; obliques make letters look alike. Source: W3C alreq; thoughtbot obliques <https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/>
- **R7** (guideline) Never style or border individual Arabic letters (search highlighting, input masks); highlight whole words. Applies: Search, forms. Evidence: Joined letters must not be separated by borders (W3C alreq). Source: W3C alreq <https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/>
- **R8** (practitioner) Budget 20 to 30% extra width for Arabic strings; never put text inside images; one typeface family (Readex Pro) for both scripts. Applies: Nav, buttons, tabs, badges. Evidence: A 10-letter English word may become a 2 to 3 word Arabic phrase; 55% of Saudi sites mixed fonts, treated as a usability problem. Source: freeCodeCamp Arabic UX; Namoun & Alkhodre <https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/designing-for-the-arab-user-basic-arabic-ux-for-business-6ff29d4c7c60/>
- **R9** (research) In Arabic, the reading-start corner is top-right: CTA, doctor name, price and next slot go there (flipped F). Applies: Home, results, card. Evidence: RTL readers follow a flipped F pattern; KSU eyetracking: Arabic users scanned results right to left, attention on the top two. Source: NN/g F-Pattern; Al-Rashed 2010 KSU <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/>
- **R10** (research) Usability-test mirrored controls (sliders, steppers, calendars) with Gulf users; a native Arabic speaker proofreads rendered screens, not strings. Applies: Prototype review, QA. Evidence: Survey of 234 bidi speakers: preferences often did not match vendor guidelines; broken joins and reversed numbers only show when rendered. Source: Goldenberg & Tractinsky 2021; freeCodeCamp <https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08070>
- **R11** (research) Charts: numerals LTR, axis order mirrored by hand; do not trust library defaults. Applies: Admin dashboards, summary timeline. Evidence: 11 Arabic-speaking practitioners: RTL support in chart tools is fragmented. Source: Alebri et al. 2026 <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24571>
